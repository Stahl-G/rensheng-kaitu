    How it works

    Keep the record. Keep things moving.

    1. 1

      Bring it in and see it

      First choose where your notes live: this Mac, iCloud, Google Drive or Nutstore. Then bring memories over from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Doubao or another AI you use, or paste a conversation. After you review each item, your goals appear on Today with their source. No API key needed.

    2. 2

      One step forward each week

      Give every goal one next step. When it's done, write one line about how it went. If it can't happen, move it to later or note why it's paused.

    3. 3

      Any AI can pick it up

      Copy a context pack to the AI you use, paste its answer back, review, then update. Ask keeps every AI service you add, so you can switch any time. Local clients like Claude and Codex read and write over MCP; AIs that can open Google Drive, such as Grok, can take over from there.

    A step forward every week

    Every goal comes down to a next step and a real result. The "Last 5 weeks" grid on the Health page lays out your training day by day, so you can see at a glance when you trained and how much.

    Your notes

    Your notes live in your own folder.

    Everything is Markdown and JSON that any editor can open. Every write saves a version, so mistakes can be undone.

    Keep your notes on this computer only, or sync them with iCloud, Google Drive, Nutstore or another sync app. Version history and keys always stay on this computer.

    Inspiration

    Where the idea came from

    “Sun-ology”, “thin muscle”, the “yellow-hair theory”… In the widely debated four-hour interview between Allen Shao and Justin Sun, three cryptic-sounding ideas were stitched together into a life philosophy about the mind, the body and action.

    Interestingly, the most concrete part of the interview is exactly how many of us have already started using AI in everyday life.

    Handing before-and-after meal photos to Claude to count calories, tracking sleep with an Oura ring, letting AI sort training videos and log weights and sets, getting a report every day. Every record is kept in local Markdown files, so you can switch to another agent and keep going.

    We have grown used to handing more and more to AI: talking through a plot while writing, weighing options when changing jobs, planning a route before a trip, working through a hard problem together.

    The more we talk, the more AI knows about what we have done, what we want to do and what we have hesitated over. But all of that is scattered across separate chat histories. What happened to last month’s plan? Why did we turn down that opportunity? What has stayed stuck lately? Finding out usually means digging through old chats again.

    So we wondered: if we gathered those scattered pieces together, could we get a life progress map of our own?

    You can look back on the road behind you. Where to go next is yours to decide, like opening up the map in a game.

    FAQ

    Does this website read my notes?

    No. This is the project website. The app on this page shows sample data and isn't connected to your notes folder. There are no analytics or third-party scripts; only the download page asks GitHub for the latest version.

    Do I have to use a particular AI?

    No. Add a key for DeepSeek, OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, Kimi, Zhipu or another service to use Ask; every service you add is kept, so you can switch. Or skip keys entirely: copy a context pack to the AI you already use and paste its answer back.

    Is it free? Is it open source?

    Free. The code is open source under the MIT License, on GitHub.

    Is this a stable release?

    Not yet. The current version is 0.1.0-rc.4, a release candidate marked as a pre-release on GitHub.

    Yes. The current version is 0.1.0-rc.4.

    Is there a Windows version?

    Yes. Download the Windows EXE, install it, and add an API key in Ask. Linux and Intel Macs can use the DSH plugin. See the download page.

    Is the app available in English?

    Yes. The interface follows your system language by default, and you can switch between 中文 and English in Settings (avatar menu, top right). Some pages are still partly in Chinese.

    Where are my notes stored?

    The first time you open Kaitu, you choose where your notes live: this computer (~/kaitu by default), iCloud, Google Drive, Nutstore, or a folder another sync app syncs. Nothing syncs unless you pick a cloud drive, and version history and keys always stay on this computer. For what is sent to an AI, see Where your data goes.
